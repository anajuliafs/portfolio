Overview

Selling energy in Brazil's free market means a long chain of data, pricing, approvals and signatures. EDP's sales team was closing these deals by stitching together fragments of the CRM, spreadsheets and e-mail. Negócio 2.0 puts the whole sale in one guided record, with fewer stages, data carried over from earlier steps and a clear view of where each deal stands.

Dynamics 365 — B2B Energia · Negócio 2.0
01 — Challenge

A sale split into
too many pieces

To contract energy on the free market, a sales rep qualifies the client, configures a package, prices it, sends an indicative proposal, collects documents, gets internal approval and manages the contract signatures. In the existing CRM, each of these moments lived in a different place.

Reps re-entered the same data for every branch of a client, checked prices against e-mails, calculated margins in Excel and asked on Teams who had approved or signed. Some stages had no real action: reps just clicked through them. Others locked the deal, so a small change meant starting over.

We wanted one linear journey, with everything the rep needs in one place and every step, approval and signature visible.

Key Insight

Reps didn't trust the system. They kept their own notes, spreadsheets and checks because the CRM didn't show what was saved, what was missing or what came next.

03 — Research

What the reps
told us

We gave each stage of the journey its own board: the goal of the stage, pain points, insights and what users said. Then we compared the stages to see which kind of pain showed up where.

Registration was the biggest technical bottleneck: duplicated data, required fields that made no sense and no data carried over between lead, account and deal. A simple flow could take 30 to 40 minutes, and a client with ten branches meant repeating it ten times.

04 — Restructuring

From ten stages
to five

Several stages added clicks without adding anything for the rep. Screening repeated the registration checks, sending the indicative offer was just a status change, and any adjustment during negotiation required a new proposal and a new approval.

We rebuilt the journey around what the rep actually does: build the proposal, get it approved, collect signatures, validate documents and close. Everything else became a tab inside the same record.

05 — Solution

One record,
one clear path

The new deal record has a stage bar at the top, so the rep always knows where the sale is and what comes next. When the proposal has all the identification, pricing and product data, it goes to internal approval. Once approved, it moves to signatures automatically.

All the information the rep needs is grouped into tabs inside the same deal:

ProposalTrackingCredit analysisContract signaturesDocumentsInternal approvalBilling dataOther

Design principles

Progressive disclosure

Ask for and show only what each step needs. Less on screen means fewer errors.

Human-centered efficiency

Based on ISO 9241-210: help the rep get the job done with less effort, and cut steps that don't help the sale.

Governance-oriented design

Design for the rep, and also for audit, traceability, legal compliance and clear ownership of each decision.

Data that fills itself

Supply startToday + 180 days, rounded to the first day of the next month.
Supply endSupply start + 3 years.
Energy dataInherited from the lead, with the option to edit.
Partner fieldsShown only when the sale involves a commercial partner.
Reference dataRead-only fields used for audit and integration, grouped in their own section, out of the rep's way.
